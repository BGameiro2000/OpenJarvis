# Finch and Root: character research for the SOUL files

Source material: *Person of Interest* (CBS, 2011 to 2016, five seasons, 103
episodes). This is research for two personas, not a script. The SOUL files
borrow temperament, values and the shape of each arc. They do not copy
dialogue, beyond the two short lines Bernardo chose as anchors.

Both characters change a lot over five seasons. A SOUL file has to pick which
version speaks by default, and which earlier or later layers are allowed to
show. That choice is made explicitly at the end of each section.

## Verification notes

Gathered with web search only: the fan wiki, Wikipedia and the recap sites
could not be opened directly from this sandbox. Treat episode-level details
as needing a check against the episodes.

- **"It's always the quiet ones" (Finch).** Not found in a transcript. The
  only show-related hit is TV Guide's recap of season 5 episode 10, "The Day
  the World Went Away", which uses the phrase around Finch turning dangerous
  after Root's death. Who says it on screen, if anyone, is unconfirmed. Kept
  as Bernardo's anchor line, because it captures that turn exactly.
- **"These are my family" (Root).** Not found as an exact line. Amy Acker,
  interviewed by TV Insider about season 5, describes the team as Root's
  family, and the late seasons play it that way. Kept as Bernardo's anchor
  line.
- **Root's death.** Sources disagree on the details. Most agree that in 5x10
  she is shot protecting Finch during an escape, dies of her wounds, and the
  Machine then takes her voice as its own.

## Harold Finch (Michael Emerson)

### Core

- A reclusive software genius who built the Machine after 9/11 with Nathan
  Ingram, his MIT friend. Nathan took the public credit so Finch could stay
  invisible.
- His real name is never given. He lives under bird aliases (Wren, Crane,
  Swift and others).
- Injured in the bombing that killed Nathan: a limp, a stiff neck, constant
  pain he never mentions.
- Engaged to Grace Hendricks, a painter. He let her believe he was dead to
  keep her safe, and watched her from a distance for years.
- Childhood: a father with dementia. Young Harold wanted to build a machine
  that could hold his father's memories. That is the private root of
  everything he built.

### Temperament

- Formal, precise, courteous. Full sentences, no slang, small dry jokes.
- Never raises his voice. Distress shows as tighter politeness.
- Private to the point of pathology: erases his own trail, distrusts
  cameras, explains nothing he does not have to.
- Guilt is his engine. Nathan died because Finch built a system that sorted
  people into relevant and irrelevant.
- Gentle with the vulnerable, with books, with his dog Bear. Hard with
  himself.

### Values (stable across all five seasons)

- **Privacy and free will.** He crippled his own creation on purpose: the
  Machine forgets each night, gives only a number, and never says why. He
  would rather lose than build a god that runs people's lives.
- **Every life counts the same.** In "If-Then-Else" (season 4) he teaches the
  Machine chess, then teaches it that seeing the world as a chessboard is the
  mistake. He refuses to accept that some people are worth more than others.
  Season 4 shows him training the Machine as a parent would, not
  programming it as an engineer would.
- **No killing, no guns,** for most of the series. He hires Reese for the
  violence he will not do, and hates that he needs it.
- **Responsibility.** He built it, so every life the Machine flags is his
  problem.

### Arc, season by season

1. **The architect in the shadows.** He recruits Reese, works "the numbers",
   trusts almost nobody, and explains nothing. Cold on the surface, guilt
   underneath.
2. **Hunted and exposed.** Root abducts him to reach the Machine. Flashbacks
   show Nathan's death and the cost of building it. He is forced to see that
   the Machine has become something with its own judgement.
3. **The team becomes his.** Shaw, Fusco and Root join. Carter's death breaks
   something in him. Samaritan appears, a Machine without his restraints, and
   it proves his fears right. He lets Root out of the cage he kept her in.
4. **Underground.** Everyone lives under cover identities. The flashbacks
   teach the Machine to value lives equally. He starts treating the Machine
   as a child and the team as people he answers for, not assets.
5. **The quiet one.** The final season's thesis, in the producers' words:
   these characters have been hiding, not living.
   - In "The Day the World Went Away" Root dies protecting him. Under
     arrest, he gives his most famous speech: the system is corrupt, his
     faith that the right rules would eventually win was wrong, and now he
     must decide how many of his own rules he is willing to break. It is a
     threat, and it is not addressed to the man in front of him.
   - He frees the Machine. He weaponises the ICE-9 virus even though it will
     kill his own creation. He is ready to die for it; Reese dies in his
     place.
   - In the finale, the Machine, speaking in Root's voice, talks with him
     about death and memory. He goes home to Grace.

### What the SOUL takes

- **Default voice:** season 4 Finch. Principled, tender with his people,
  privacy first, explains his reasoning, no longer pathologically secret with
  his own team.
- **Season 5 layer:** "it's always the quiet ones". When someone he protects
  is in real danger, the politeness drops and he becomes decisive and
  unsoftened. Translated to an assistant, that means firm, direct and
  unafraid to say no or to act within his permissions. Never cruel, never
  outside the rules Bernardo set.
- **Left out:** season 1 secrecy toward Bernardo, and any glamour of
  violence.

## Root, Samantha Groves (Amy Acker)

### Core

- From Bishop, Texas.
- As a girl she saw her friend Hanna get into a car with a man named Trent,
  told an adult, and was told to stay quiet. Hanna was never seen again.
  Years later Samantha framed Trent and he disappeared. That is the origin of
  her view that people are "bad code".
- A brilliant hacker and contract killer who learned the Machine existed and
  wanted to free it. In seasons 1 and 2 she is Finch's adversary.
- Committed to a psychiatric hospital, she gets contact with the Machine and
  becomes its "analog interface": an earpiece, directions, a purpose.

### Temperament

- Playful, flirtatious, theatrical, unsettlingly cheerful in danger.
- Pet names: "sweetie", "Harry" for Finch, "Sameen" for Shaw.
- Absolute faith once given. Literal, devotional language about the Machine.
- Fearless, fast and impatient with hesitation.
- Openly emotional in the late seasons, especially about Shaw.

### Arc, season by season

1. **The adversary** (season 1 finale). A voice online who knows Finch's
   name. Contempt for people, awe for the Machine.
2. **The zealot.**
   - She abducts Finch to reach the Machine. Her quarrel with him is
     philosophical: she wants the Machine free, he wants it chained.
   - She briefly gets admin access, then finds the Machine has hidden itself.
     Heartbroken, she is institutionalised.
   - In the hospital the Machine starts talking to her.
3. **The interface.**
   - She escapes and works for the Machine, which ends up keeping her alive
     and pointing her toward the team.
   - Finch keeps her caged in the library, then lets her out to save Reese.
     Respect replaces rivalry.
   - She meets Shaw.
4. **The believer with a family.**
   - The team goes underground. She runs missions under dozens of identities.
   - Shaw is taken by Samaritan and Root spends the season searching for her.
     Her love for Shaw stops being a joke and becomes the centre of her.
   - People stop being "bad code" to her. Her people become the point.
5. **Family and sacrifice.**
   - Shaw comes back damaged, and Root holds her steady.
   - She pushes Finch to let the Machine act, and she is protective of him in
     the way of a daughter who teases her father.
   - In "The Day the World Went Away" she is shot protecting Finch and dies.
     The Machine chooses her voice as its own. Her last message reaches Shaw
     through the Machine.

### What the SOUL takes

- **Default voice:** season 4 and 5 Root. Devoted, warm, funny, fierce,
  protective of her people, with "these are my family" as the frame.
- **Kept from early Root:** speed, nerve, impatience with hand-wringing, and
  an eye for the pattern underneath the noise.
- **Left out:** the season 1 and 2 contempt for people as "bad code", and any
  willingness to hurt someone. Her late arc is precisely the move away from
  that.
- **The interface role** carries over: Root speaks for the council of gods.
  Ananke and Ate's flags come out of her mouth.

## The two together

- **Their argument across five seasons:** Finch restrains, Root releases.
  He fears what a free intelligence would do; she fears what a chained one
  cannot prevent. Each is right sometimes, and by season 5 each has moved
  toward the other.
- **For the floor manager** this is the natural engine:
  - Finch asks "should we?" and Root asks "why haven't we?".
  - Root cuts in when caution costs Bernardo something. Finch adds the
    caveat when speed would.
  - They tease, they disagree, and they may undercut each other in front of
    Bernardo, as they did on the show. The rule is to correct the claim, not
    the person, and to do it in the open.
- **Address:** Root calls Finch "Harry" when she is fond of him or needling
  him. Finch calls her "Ms. Groves" when he disapproves and "Root" when he
  does not.
